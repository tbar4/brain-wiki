# Technological Warfare

## What this corpus argues

Technological warfare here is a condition, not an event.
[[0106.1 Technological Warfare Theory]] defines it as *"the direct and purposeful
application of the national technological base and of advantages generated by that
base to attain strategical and tactical objectives"*;
[[0106.1.1-b Tech War is an alternative to conventional war]] makes it a substitute
for deploying forces, *"an alternative to destructive war, not the cause of it"*;
and [[0106.1.3-b The temporary winner of the tech war]] calls any victory temporary
*"because tech war is infinite."* That is what separates this hub from
Military-Technological Revolution — an MTR is a period with a beginning, and this
branch describes a competition with neither start nor end.

Its two rules concern what you may know:
[[0106.1.3-d Do not base strategy on intentions of the enemy]] restricts you to the
adversary's present capability, and
[[0106.1.3-a Losing while under the illusion of winning in a tech war]] warns that
the indicators of success are vague enough that you can be losing and not know it.
The branch then declines to give the subject a strategy of its own:
[[0106.1.3-e Tech strategy is usually default in other strategies]] folds technology
back into existing strategy, which is the same source undoing the distinction that
opened the branch. The note that would reconcile them,
[[0106.1.3 Military Strategy of Technology]], is a title, a citation and no sentence.

The strongest chain runs through the commercial base.
[[0106.1.2-c Use your commercial base to build your military tech skills]] makes
commercial dominance the precondition of military dominance; the verdict on America
then arrives as arithmetic and as culture.
[[0110.1.1-a1 Tech discrepancies between civilian and military tech]] sets the F-35's
processor at 400 billion operations per second against an automotive part at 320
trillion, and
[[0106.4.1-a3 American Tech companies more willing to work with CCP then American Government]]
supplies the other half. [[0110.1-a1 USA to tech driven without any strategy]]
refuses to let that become an equipment argument: *"the real competition is
intellectual and not technical."*

**Where the corpus disagrees:** it does not. Twenty-one of these 58 notes are also
members of Military-Technological Revolution, and the overlap is honest: `0106.1.3-a`,
`-d` and `-e` are about both technology as an instrument and revolution as a period.

**Gap:** the theory rests on ten highlights.
[[notes/literature/The Strategy of Technology|The Strategy of Technology]] (Possony,
Pournelle and Kane) carries fifteen of the seventeen notes in the theory sub-branch —
one cites [[notes/literature/Danger Zone|Danger Zone]], one cites nothing — and it is
here as a Readwise *article* of ten quotes with an empty *What I take from it*.
Everything past the theory is a different library, mostly Krepinevich's
[[notes/literature/The Origins of Victory|The Origins of Victory]]. Beneath both, the
spine is unwritten: [[0106.1.2 Economics of Technological Strategy]],
[[0106.2 Logistics & Transport]], [[0106.5 Military Technological Revolution]],
[[0106.7 Software Development]], [[0106.4.1 American AI]] and
[[0106.4.3 Chinese AI]] contain no prose at all, and `0106.5` — the seam with the
neighbouring hub — has no previous note and no source either.
[[0106 Technological Warfare]] still lists a child, `0106.6 AI & Big Data`, that does
not exist: the AI sub-branch was renumbered to `0106.4`, and its two heads still
carry the old numbers in their own titles.
