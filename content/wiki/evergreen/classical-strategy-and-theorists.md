# Classical Strategy and Theorists

## What this corpus argues

The premise is stated once and never revisited. [[0100 The Art of Strategy]] holds that strategy "will always remain an art" — too many variables, too much chance, never a precise science — and everything downstream behaves as though that settles it. What follows is not a search for laws but a case file, and it is filed by *who said it*: the named theorist is this hub's organising unit.

Two carry it. *[[On War]]* supplies 33 notes and *[[The Prince]]* 22, and they are read differently. Clausewitz is read for how he thinks — [[0102.2 Clausewitz]] takes from him that every commander must be his own theorist, and what follows is friction, moral force and [[0102.2.6 Friction in War|what separates real war from war on paper]] rather than maxims. Machiavelli is read structurally: [[0102.4-a The Themes of Machiavelli in "The Prince"]] cuts the book into three — conflict is inevitable, pacify the conquered, then rule once power is consolidated — and all 22 notes hang off that scaffold. Beside them [[0102.3 Jomini]] is one sentence naming him Clausewitz's "polar opposite."

The one place a theorist is weighed against a method is Trevor's own: [[0101.1.1 Validating Theory]] sets Clausewitz's descriptive teaching beside Jomini's prescriptive guide and concludes you should employ both. [[0103 Indirect Warfare]] runs the other way — five of its seven notes are Liddell Hart's *[[Strategy]]*, all historical cases rather than doctrine, and [[0103.1 Indirect Approach doesn't always win the day]] is a counter-example against the branch's own title.

**Where the corpus disagrees:** almost nowhere, and the near-miss is worth naming. [[0102.2.2-d Clausewitz writing is insight into how he thinks]] says that following the maxim "attack where the opponent is weakest" would get you destroyed by Napoleon ten times out of ten, and attributes that maxim to Sun Tzu. It is the only sentence in the vault rating one theorist against another's advice — and there is otherwise no note about Sun Tzu at all. *Sun Tzu in Space* is cited 40 times in the vault, but 39 land next door in [[0104 International Relations|the IR branch]]; it is a textbook on international politics, not this hub's book.

**Gap:** fifteen notes carry no prose, and they are the joints. [[0102 Military Theorists]] and [[0102.4 Niccolo Machiavelli]] are bare link lists; [[0102.2.4 The Politics of War]] and [[0102.2.5 Defeating the Enemy]] are empty headings above notes that quote *On War* by page. One branch is worse than empty: [[0108 Logistics & Supply Chain]] is misnamed. Its four notes are procurement bureaucracy out of *[[The Technological Republic]]* ([[0108.1-b Military suffer from "Brute Force" thinking|$25bn of armour against $300 IEDs]]), and none is about logistics or a supply chain.
