## What this corpus argues

In these notes AI is a data-supply problem before it is a model problem.
[[0106.4-a Big Data will be the bottleneck in AI]] puts the decisive factor in the
rate at which data reaches the models — *"from compression algorithms to
communications protocols"* — rather than in the models themselves.
[[0105.3.1 Communications will transition from human to human into computer to computer]]
follows that to its end state: volume pushes the analyst out until communication is
machine-to-machine *"with AI making decisions."*
[[0106.4.1-a1 Big Data, AI, and how to win Scouting War]] then turns the pipe into
the target — *"corrupting and manipulating data and enemy systems to feed bad inputs
for their analysis."* Every failure mode named here is a data failure.

The American material is diagnosis and programme, never doctrine.
[[0106.4.1-a1 DoD doesn't treat the data it generates properly, views most of it as waste]]
has the Pentagon treating data *"like engine exhaust"* and meeting the volume by
adding people rather than machine learning.
[[0106.4.1-a Project Maven to solve Big Data Issues?]] names Maven and the NRO's
Sentient, [[0106.4.1-b Open source efforts improve military tech]] names swarm
software, [[1005.5 AI in the USSF]] names anti-jam rerouting — programmes, with no
theory of how any of them wins — and
[[0106.4.1-c American's don't trust AI]] puts a public ceiling on all of it.

China gets the theory. [[0105.3.2.1 The Chinese "Systems Destruction Warfare"]] gives
a five-subsystem model of an operational system and puts the competition in
deconstructing the enemy's reconnaissance and strike complex;
[[0105.3.2-a Chinese Journal predicts future war will be fought in space and cyber]]
has the PLA's *Science of Military Strategy* moving war *"from systems confrontation
to algorithms"* in 2013; [[0110.2.1 The Assassin's Mace]] is that idea in weapon
form. That asymmetry is the hub's real content: one side has a named account of how
AI decides a war, the other has budgets and complaints.

**Where the corpus disagrees:** it does not, and here the silence is specific.
[[0106.4.3-b China investing in Autonomous Weapons technology]] records that China
backed a ban on fully autonomous lethal weapons in April 2018 while developing them
faster than any other military — and nothing answers it. No note in this hub asks
whether a machine should be permitted to make a lethal decision; *human-in-the-loop*
appears nowhere in the permanent tree, and `0105.3.1` has AI making the decisions as
a matter of course.

**Gap:** the subject was never given an address, and the tags standing in for one cut
across it both ways. [[0105.3 Battle Networks]] — the vault's only definition of a
battle network, and the source of the *"close the kill chain"* formula this hub is
named for — carries neither `ai` nor `big-data` and is not a member; nor is
[[0105.3.3 DoD has poor technical networks (i.e. data networks)]]. The same tags
admit two notes with no military content at all:
[[SaaS is dead and teaching how to build AI tools is in]] and
[[Understanding the AI Infrastructure Ecosystem - From Models to MCP]], a
career-context explainer of models, agents and MCP. And both heads are empty —
[[0106.4.1 American AI]] and [[0106.4.3 Chinese AI]] are tables with no rows, still
titled `0106.6.1` and `0106.6.2` under a parent, `0106.6 AI & Big Data`, that no
longer exists.
