## What this corpus argues

This hub exists because of an accounting failure. `political-theory` is the vault's second-heaviest tag, and no cluster could hold it: 44 of the notes carrying it matched nothing, and the 33 that carry it and nothing else were, in practice, invisible. They are not miscellany. They are one discipline read out of one book — 39 of these 69 notes cite *[[Sun Tzu in Space]]* (Gregory D. Miller), a book about international politics *in space*, so the theory arrives already bent toward one application. [[0104.4.4 Offensive-Defensive realist theory]] closes by ruling that the offensive currently has the advantage in orbit.

What separates this from [[0100 The Art of Strategy|the strategy branch]] is the unit of analysis, and the filing shows it: that branch is organised by named theorist, this one names almost nobody. Across the realism notes not one thinker appears — the schools are named, the people are not. The working definition is Miller's assumption list in [[0104.1-b Realism is based off several assumptions]]: states as unitary, self-interested rational actors under anarchy, long-term cooperation unlikely. The branch root [[0104.1 Realism]] does *not* say this. It defines realism as the proper use of force within laws and regulations producing peace for all — word-for-word what its own child [[0104.1-e Soft Realism]] gives as the definition of **soft** realism, both from *[[Toward a Theory of Spacepower]]*. The root names the branch after a thing it never defines.

Three notes are Trevor arguing rather than transcribing. Of the four notes in the whole 550-note slip box whose reference reads `Self` rather than a book, three are here. [[0104-b Duality of IR Policy in the US]] prescribes realism toward China, Russia, Iran and North Korea and liberalism toward allies; [[0104.2-a Technological S-Curves affect IR]] makes that choice a function of where you sit on the curve; [[0104.4.1-a Hegemon's need economic power]] runs hegemony through transport technology — Roman roads, British ships, American aircraft, then space. All three convert a described theory into a policy rule keyed to technology. That move is the hub's own claim, and it is not Miller's.

**Where the corpus disagrees:** it does not, and the reason is structural. Realism and liberalism are set against each other repeatedly — on human nature, on international organisations, on collective security — but always inside one author's exposition, which supplies both sides. Even [[0104.1-c Realism has several flaws]] is Miller criticising realism. No second author is present to contest him.

**Gap:** the theories are catalogued and almost never used. [[0104.8 Constructivism]] is one note applied to nothing; feminism has two. Nine notes carry no prose and every one is a category name rather than a claim — [[0104.3 Anarchy]], [[0104.4 Hegemony]] and [[0104.10 Treaties]] are empty headings sitting above written children.
