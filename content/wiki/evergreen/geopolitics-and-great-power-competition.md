# Geopolitics and Great-Power Competition

## What this corpus argues

This is the hub about a particular rivalry, which is what separates it from [[0104 International Relations|the IR theory branch]] next door. That branch asks how states behave in general and names almost nobody; this one names Mackinder, Spykman, Kennan and China, and asks who is winning.

Half of it is a reading of *[[The Eurasian Century]]*, which supplies ten of the twelve citations in branch 0107 and is the only route by which the classical geopoliticians enter the vault at all: [[0107-a Halford Mackinder founded Geopolitics|Mackinder]] arrives with the world island, the Eurasian heartland and the outer rim, and with the [[0107-a1 Mackinder predicts Chinese rise|prediction that a properly administered China would become a world threat]]; [[0107-b Spykman -> States Survive because of Power Politics|Spykman]] arrives with an anarchic world in which power politics always wins. Neither is read directly.

The argument built on them is consistent: American withdrawal is the danger, not American overreach. [[0107.1-a Historical & Neo-Isolationist America leads to chaos]] runs the 1930s non-interventionists forward and says modern Trumpian neo-isolationism treads the same path; [[0107.4-a Desire for Peace can lead to war]] blames the interwar allies' restraint rather than the Versailles terms. Its positive form is [[0107.4-b World needs a "community of power"]] — not a balance of power but a hegemonic community acting in liberal democracy's interest — and its instrument is economic, [[0107.3-a Economic Sanctions stronger than the gun]].

The China half is where the hub stops being theory, and it is thinner than its count. Five notes make one case, that WTO entry let China cheat: state enterprises at 40% of GDP, [[2201.1a China entry into World Trade Organization bad for Americans|2.4 million American jobs]], [[2201.b America losing to China in trade growth|$107bn in sales lost to IP theft]]. Only the two unnumbered notes reason rather than tally, and [[2200.? Chinese Theoretical Attack on US]] is the sharpest thing here — a full PLA campaign (A2/AD, then networks, markets and media, deliberately leaving America an out) built out of Sun Tzu and Clausewitz together. It is the one note in the vault that puts [[0102.2 Clausewitz|classical theory]] to work on a live competitor.

**Where the corpus disagrees:** it does not, and on China it barely speaks. The 21-note China branch carries exactly one citation in total — *[[Danger Zone]]*, in a single note. Everything else there asserts.

**Gap:** that branch is an outline of a book never read into it. Fourteen of its 21 notes contain no prose at all — [[2208 Mao]], [[2205 Chinese Subversion of US]], [[2207 American Intelligence Failures]], [[2212 Principles for 100 Year Marathon]], [[2213 Beating China]] and nine more are titles above an empty *Related Notes* table. The headings name their source, and *[[The Hundred-Year Marathon]]* is in the vault as a literature note holding 16 highlights, cited by no permanent note anywhere. The scaffold came from a table of contents; the reading never landed. Filling it is what would give this half an argument instead of an inventory.
