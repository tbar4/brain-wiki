# Military-Technological Revolution

*evergreen · 82 notes · 3 core tags*

## What this corpus argues

The corpus holds one thesis, and it is stated in Trevor's own words rather
than quoted: the era is technologically disruptive and American leadership has
failed to meet it. [[0101.1.8 We are in a Military Revolution and US leaders are failing]]
calls that leadership *"slow, corrupt, and misinformed and too bureaucratic"*
and argues that militaries which disrupt share the characteristics of
businesses that do — sourced to Krepinevich, [[The Origins of Victory]] p.443.
Most of what follows is a variation on that one failure: platforms bought
instead of networks, technology adopted without a strategy, commercial
industry outrunning the services that need it.

Two accounts of *why* it fails sit in the same branch. [[The Kill Chain]]
(Brose) puts the failure in systems and procurement — the wrong things are
being bought, and the network rather than the platform is the weapon.
[[Net Assessment and Military Strategy]]
(Mahnken and Marshall) puts it one step earlier, in diagnosis: the competition
is misread before anything is bought. That second line is the one
[[0101.2b Andrew Marshall's Theory - Out-Think, not Out-Spend]] draws from
Marshall's own practice — out-think rather than out-spend — and
[[0109.2.2 The Characteristics of Net Assessment]] sets out the method that
follows from it: descriptive rather than prescriptive, built on asymmetry and
imperfect information, and measured in decades.

That method's account here comes almost entirely from its own founder. Branch
0109 cites two works, and 18 of its 19 citations are the volume Marshall
co-authored. No critical or external evaluation of net assessment appears
anywhere in this vault, so the method is described only by the people who
practise it.

**Where the corpus disagrees:** it does not. The procurement reading and the
diagnostic reading are both present and were never put to each other — no note
here cites one against the other, and nothing answers whether buying better or
seeing better comes first. That is an unasked question, not a settled one, and
it holds vault-wide: no source in this corpus argues with another.

**Gap:** the roots are empty. [[0110 Revolution in Military Affairs]] and
[[0109 Net Assessment]] — the heads of the two densest branches in the vault —
contain no prose at all. Each is an H1, four child links and an empty *Related
Notes* table; neither carries a single sentence saying what the thing is.
Everything above was assembled from the leaves, because the root was never
written. That absence is what this hub exists to fill.
