# Western Decline and Culture

*Draft header — written from the corpus, awaiting Trevor's revision.*

*evergreen · 26 notes · branch 2100, two books, one direction*

## What this corpus argues

The claim is that the West is under attack from inside its own institutions, and
two books make it. Douglas Murray's *The War on the West* supplies the whole of
2101.1. [[2101.1 Attacks on Western Culture]] holds that attacking the West as
racist is now the academic default and that critical race theory supplies the
measure, judging races rather than individuals;
[[2101.1.a Critical Race Theory]] dates it to the universities of the 1970s and
puts its core move as *racism = prejudice plus power*, which makes only white
people capable of racism, its vocabulary read as *1984* doublespeak in
[[2101.1.a1 The Double Speak of CRT]];
[[2101.1.a3 CRT Warps perceptions of Society]] measures the effect, citing a
survey in which 22% of very liberal respondents put annual police killings of
unarmed black Americans above 10,000 against an actual figure under 10; and
[[2101.1.c Why does only the west need to change its demographic?]] states the
general form — the West is held to a standard no other society is held to.

Karp and Zamiska's *The Technological Republic* supplies 2101.3, where the
subject is discourse rather than race.
[[2101.3.1 Elites are Cosmopolitan, People are local]] separates a class insulated
by wealth from people exposed to what happens around them,
[[2101.3.2 American Public Arena doesn't allow discourse]] argues that leaders can
no longer think aloud so sensitivity displaces belief,
[[2101.3.2a All beliefs can't be equally valid and true]] sets Enlightenment
objectivity against postmodern relativism, and
[[2101.4 America's software engineers are virtue signaling morons]] turns that on
Silicon Valley, which will not build defence systems while depending on the
institution that protects it. One note is Hal Brands's
([[2101.2.a Collapse of European Empires Radicalized the Global South]]); one is
footnoted *"Self"*.

**Where the corpus disagrees:** it does not, and for this cluster that is the
central fact. No note states the case for critical race theory, post-colonialism
or the cosmopolitan position in its own terms. The opponents are named and not
read: Ibram X. Kendi's *How to Be an Antiracist* sits in the library at
`sources/readwise/Supplementals/How to Be an Antiracist` with zero highlights, no
literature note and no citation anywhere in the vault, while
[[2101.1.a4 How to be Anti-Racists Effect on Society]] — the note describing that
book — reaches it through Murray and attributes it to Robin D'Angelo. Edward Said
appears once, in
[[2101.3.1a Many Elites have no idea theirs ideas com from Edward Said]], as the
unacknowledged origin of an error.

**Gap:** the roots and the sourcing. [[2101.2 Post-Colonialism]] and
[[2101.3 Elitism]] hold no prose, and [[2101 Western Culture]] is two links that
omit Elitism, though Elitism names it as its parent. Three notes cannot be
checked: [[2101.1.b3 Communism is a malignant parasite]] is footnoted with a name
and no work, and the two China notes carry hard figures with no footnote at all.
And [[2101.? Christianity and War]] still has a literal `?` where its identifier
belongs — never numbered in, tagged for religion, and about just war rather than
decline.
