## What this corpus argues

The corpus is realist by conviction, not by survey. [[1007.1 Realpolitik in Space]]
assumes an anarchic order and concludes weaponisation is inevitable, and
[[1004.5 Second to Weaponize Space]] turns that into an American imperative: the
first to weaponise controls LEO, and being second means needing that power's
consent. The alternatives are present and inert:
[[1007.2 Neoliberal Theory in Space]] and [[1007.6 Constructivist Theory in Space]]
are one defining sentence each, both sourced to the same volume the realist notes
cite. [[1007.1.4 Naivety in International Space Relations]] then attacks the
cooperative reading at length — against an opponent this vault states in a single
sentence.

Two definitions of spacepower share one paragraph of [[1001 Spacepower Theory]]:
the broad *"ability in peace, crisis, and war to exert prompt and sustained
influence in or from space"* from [[notes/literature/Toward a Theory of Spacepower|Toward a Theory of Spacepower]], and Joshua
Carlson's military-only one from [[notes/literature/Spacepower Ascendant|Spacepower Ascendant]]. The note calls the
second *"a little more strict"* and moves on. Nothing chooses, and the choice is
load-bearing: it decides whether [[1008.3 Private Sector importance in Realist Theory]]
and the astronautics branch describe spacepower or only its preconditions.

On America the diagnosis is never about technology:
[[1005.3 American Technology vs Chinese Strategy]] has the US keeping pace
technically and losing on vision.
[[1001.5 The Role of Technology in Spacepower]] is the one note that adds to the
theory rather than recording it, arguing technology deserves its own *logic delta*
beside the military, economic and political ones.

**Where the corpus disagrees:** once, and it is recorded rather than manufactured.
[[1001.1 "Silent Sea"]] sets a *"Dolman style brown water"* school against a
*"Carlsonian or Ziarnick blue-water"* one,
[[1001.1-a Blue Water Spacepower School of Thought]] and
[[1001.1-b Brown Water Spacepower School of Thought]] give each its own note, and
[[1005.1 American Focus on Terrestrial War-fighting]] files current US practice as
brown-water. But both schools are described from one side: those two notes carry
empty reference lists, and [[1003.1 Everett Dolman]] is sourced to
[[notes/literature/Toward a Theory of Spacepower|Toward a Theory of Spacepower]], not to Dolman.

**Gap:** [[notes/literature/Astropolitik|Astropolitik]] is here — 392 highlights of it — `distilled: false`, its
*What I take from it* section empty, and the link `[[Astropolitik]]` occurring
exactly once in the whole permanent tree, as a related-note pointer rather than a
citation. The rival school's leader is named repeatedly and read nowhere. Beneath
that, [[1000 Spacepower]] is twelve links and no sentence, and
[[1003 Space Power Theorists]], [[1006.2 Chinese Commercial Space Power]] and
[[1006.3 Chinese Military Spacepower]] carry no prose at all.
