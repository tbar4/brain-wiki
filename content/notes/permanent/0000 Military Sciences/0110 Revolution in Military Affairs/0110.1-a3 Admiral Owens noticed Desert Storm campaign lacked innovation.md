# Admiral Owens noticed Iraq campaign lacked innovation
Admiral William Owens, who was the vice chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff a year after Desert Storm concluded "we conducted the campaign in Iraq essentially as Napoleon, Ulysses S. Grant, and Dwight D. Eisenhower had conducted their early campaigns" in other words, mass brute force. This is the antithesis of Andrew Marshall's predictions of a revolution in military affairs [^1] . Admiral Owens pointed out that we used that victory to validate doctrine, attacked, and weapons that had prevailed against a rather inept foe. He learned that if we don't change our thinking that we will see a dissolution of American strength, and perhaps a total collapse of our military capability[^2].
